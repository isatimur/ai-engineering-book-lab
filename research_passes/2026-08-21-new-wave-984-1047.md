## Six clusters, and what is actually new

### 1. Continual learning becomes a named track (6 talks)

This did not exist as a cluster in the #795–983 pass. It is the sharpest new
thing in the corpus.

- Parth Asawa, UC Berkeley (#1003) **[T]** — proposes a Continual Learning Bench built on headroom, shared structure and learning mechanism, scored on reward/gain/cost frontiers. Treats continual learning as an unsolved *measurement* problem: current benchmarks do not test for it.
- Yu Su, Neocognition (#1007) **[T]** — frames continual learning as "adaptive compression of experience" and as the bridge from *intelligence* to *expertise*, arguing for unbounded expertise from bounded intelligence.
- Sara Hooker, Adaption Labs (#1008) **[T]** — gradient-free continual learning; argues pretraining scale is exhausted. **[S]** claims sub-13B models are closing the gap on the leaderboard and that fewer than 5,000 people worldwide can train frontier models.
- Ronak Malde, Trajectory (#1004) **[T]** — on-policy self-distillation as a single-rollout per-token alternative to GRPO; at 120B+ with 100+ tool calls it develops "hint leakage", an analogue of reward hacking.
- Sam Denton, Applied Compute (#1010) **[T]** — naive SFT on production traces degraded general coding performance; targeted online-hint distillation recovered the specific behaviour 15%→80% without collapse.
- Jack Morris, Engram (#1006) **[T]** — naive fine-tuning on a fixed private corpus collapses generation quality outright.

**Two genuine disagreements, worth more than the consensus:**
1. Asawa treats continual learning as a measurement problem; Su treats it as a definitional bridge and bets algorithms matter more than further scaling. Complementary framings, different investment theses.
2. Malde bets on RL-style per-token distillation; Hooker bets on automating the whole training loop while declaring pretraining scale finished.

**Also:** Morris and Denton describe the *same* failure (training on your own outputs) with different outcomes — total collapse vs. recoverable via hint distillation. That gap is a research question, not a contradiction.

### 2. Memory: the negative result is the finding (2 talks)

- Stefania Druga, Sakana AI (#1005) **[A]** — "when your task fits in context, the harness doesn't add much." Memory/retrieval machinery added cost and nothing else until tasks outgrew the window; ranked-ledger recall then beat both no-recall and gating, on local models.
- Shlok Khemani (#1012) **[T]** — comparative anatomy of shipped memory systems: ChatGPT's ~4,000-token opaque profile against Claude's ~1,000-token visible one, plus a documented staleness bug (the system believed he had visited Turkey when he went to Thailand).

Druga's result is now the second source under **C4** — it converges with Towards AI's costed compaction experiment from the opposite direction, which is why C4 moved to moderate-to-strong.

### 3. Computer use and the web as agent substrate (5 talks)

- Pierluca D'Oro, Programma Labs (#1014) **[A]** — pass@k on deterministic environments is satisfiable by a model-free replay agent. Now claim **C5**. **[T]** also reports naive confidence intervals achieving ~17–20% real coverage against a nominal 95%.
- Corey Gallon (#1016) **[S]** — cites a study putting CLI and MCP at comparable success (~83%) but CLI at 7 turns/<1 min against MCP's 71 round trips/8 min, and reports Anthropic measuring CLI tool calls up to 75x cheaper. Striking if true; sourced to others, so verify before use.
- Dhruv Batra, Yutori (#1002) **[S]** — Navigator N1.5 at 97% on Online Mind2Web and ~$0.80/task against ~$230/task for larger frontier agents. Vendor self-report.
- Omer Primor, Bright Data (#1017) **[T]** — build-vs-rent tipping point around 15,000 entities, and the counterintuitive result that context-as-a-service underperformed general web search on coverage because it can only surface what was already collected.
- Patricija Žemaitytė, Oxylabs (#1018) **[T]** — SERP latency from ~4s to 550ms while volume grew 400M→6B daily requests.

### 4. Healthcare: an entire vertical track (8 talks)

The strongest evidence body for the book-two vertical thesis (see
`05_Book_Ideas/Book Two — Scoping from Uncovered Corpus.md`).

- Vasant Kearney, Onlay (#1035) **[A]** — "all actually agree on the wrong information": phone, portal and X12 EDI can concur that a patient is covered and the claim is denied anyway. Now claim **C6**, a counterexample to redundancy-as-reliability.
- Chaitanya Asawa, Abridge (#1030) **[T]** — deployed across ~300 US health systems against ~100M medical conversations/year; decomposes one frontier-model task into many small post-trained models per clinical-note section for cost and latency.
- Vivek Muppalla, Hippocratic AI (#1034) **[T]** — reports 99.89% "no harm" against ~81% for human clinicians on the same rubric across five generations.
- Anuj Iravane, Anterior (#1038) **[T]** — in blind review clinicians distinguished synthetic from real records only ~60% of the time, with ~90% of eval datasets now synthetic.
- Jared Joselowitz, Ufonia (#1032) **[T]** — LLM judge validated against 10 clinicians on 240 cases at F1 0.96; formatting changes alone swung benchmark scores by 76 points, motivating GEPA-based prompt optimisation.
- Rashi Agrawal, Hinge Health (#1031) **[S]** — cites a Mount Sinai finding that a consumer health AI under-triaged life-threatening emergencies 50% of the time, ECRI naming AI-chatbot misuse the #1 health technology hazard of 2026, and a bromide-poisoning case from LLM dietary advice. All third-party; verify each independently.

**Convergence:** Hinge Health and Maven Clinic independently arrive at the same
pattern — push irreversible or high-stakes decisions into deterministic code
*above* the model, and reserve multi-model agreement or human escalation for
what cannot tolerate a hallucination.

**Tension:** Hippocratic's "safer than clinicians" framing sits directly against
Anterior's insistence on human escalation and full auditability. Citing both in
one chapter needs care.

### 5. Harness engineering keeps earning its keep (4 talks)

- Anthropic, Gagan Bhat & Isabella Kai He (#1000) **[T]** — decoupling the agent's "brain" from its "hands" gave 60% faster time-to-first-token at P50 and >90% at P95. Also the cleanest **version-skew** example in the corpus: a harness fix for Sonnet 4.5's "context anxiety" became pure overhead once Opus 4.5 shipped.
- Ryan Dahl, Deno (#1020) **[T]** — model alignment is not security. A model refusing destructive commands is not a control; the guard belongs outside the agent, at the byte/protocol level rather than HTTP.
- Vivek Trivedy, LangChain (#1013) **[T]** — matched Opus-quality trace judging with a cheaper open model at one to two orders of magnitude lower cost via harness engineering.
- Ben Hylak, Raindrop (#1011) **[T]** — "floor vs ceiling" framing and eval-as-code; argues eval practice is stuck in a chatbot-era static-test-set mindset that breaks on every model or harness swap.

### 6. Context economics inverted (1 talk, but it moves a chapter)

- Towards AI (#1019) **[A]** — full history beat every compaction technique on cost, latency and recall; the compaction arm degraded to 38%. Now claim **C4**, which *complicates Chapter 5's* framing of summarisation as design restraint.

## Cluster 7 — Sandboxing converges from three directions

Independent teams arrive at the same conclusion: **the guard belongs outside the
agent**, and least privilege is the design, not a setting.

- Kenton Varda, Cloudflare (#984) **[A-verified quote]** — a double sandbox: the agent-written app runs in a null-origin iframe under strict CSP, talking only via postMessage/Cap'n Web RPC to a network-isolated Durable Object. Agent-introduced XSS has nothing to exfiltrate *to*.
- Idan Gazit, GitHub (#992) **[T]** — "Agentic Workflows": markdown automations compiling to Actions YAML, with guardrails in frontmatter (tool access, network allow-list, single-PR "safe outputs").
- Tushar Jain, Docker (#1047) **[T]** — SPX, a scoped-sandbox runtime, motivated by scope creep.
- Dominik Kundel, OpenAI (#997) **[T]** — Codex sandboxes per-platform: Seatbelt on macOS, Bubblewrap on Linux, custom on Windows.
- Sarthak Aggarwal, Decawork (#1043) **[T]** — agent identity/authorization, using EchoLeak (a CVE against Microsoft 365 Copilot) and Replit's agent deleting a production database *despite an explicit code freeze* as the case for it.

This directly extends Chapter 7. Ryan Dahl's line from the earlier batch —
alignment is not security — is the thesis; these four are the implementations.

## Cluster 8 — Generative media as an engineering discipline (8 talks)

Relevant to the book-two vertical thesis, where gen-media sits at 5% coverage.

- Krea (#1022, #1028) **[T, spot-verified]** — ~30-40 in-house classifiers and Wikipedia page-rank for world-knowledge coverage; infrastructure at 1.8 TB/s reads, sub-8-hour crash cadence, a hard 78°C GPU-throttle rule, sub-30s terabyte checkpointing.
- LemonSlice (#1026) **[T]** — denoising cut from ~30 steps to 1 for real-time avatars.
- uRun (#1027) **[S]** — cost framing of "$10 → ~3 hrs of generated video"; speaker's own figures.
- Reelful (#1023) **[T]** — an agentic video editor composing via Remotion-as-code with a verification/re-iterate loop; argues *editing* is more tractable than generating.

The engineering here is recognisably the book's own subject — harnesses,
verification loops, cost/latency budgets — applied to pixels instead of text.

## Three tensions worth more than the summaries

1. **Fine-tune vs. context-engineer.** Lease End (#1046) **[T]** reports a fine-tuned classifier that drove $12M at 50x ROI and *still* became tech debt: after rebuilding on skills/context over a frontier model, their fix cycle went from ~a week to under an hour. The compression panel (#986) and Krea argue the opposite — quantisation and distillation are exactly the right lever once cost, latency or narrow-task control bind. Both are transcript-grounded. This is the sharpest practical disagreement in the wave.
2. **Is capability solved?** The NVIDIA/Prime Intellect/Arcee panel (#987) **[T]** predicts open models closing the frontier gap; Sonar (#995), Wisedocs (#991), Amazon AGI Lab (#999) and Docker all describe error rates that still demand heavy verification. Wisedocs supplies the concrete counterweight: the same refactor took O3 three hours with 10 mistakes, Opus 4.8 one-shot, and GPT-5.5 ten minutes to produce shallow scaffolding.
3. **Agent sprawl is a leadership problem.** Amazon AGI Lab (#999) **[T]** — uncoordinated agents drove issue count to 400-500 within weeks; they cap skill files at ~100 lines and use token burn as a diagnostic (20-25k healthy, 40-50k a red flag). That is Chapter 9 material with numbers attached.
