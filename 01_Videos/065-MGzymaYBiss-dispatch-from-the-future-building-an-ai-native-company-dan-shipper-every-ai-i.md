# Dispatch from the Future: building an AI-native Company – Dan Shipper, Every, AI & I

## Summary
Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, argues there is a "10x difference" between an org where 90% of engineers use AI and one where 100% do, because even a small minority working the old way forces the whole team to accommodate traditional workflows. As evidence, he describes Every itself: 15 people running four production software products (Kora, an email assistant; Monologue, a speech-to-text app; Spiral) across six business units, growing MRR by double digits monthly for six months with over 7,000 paying and 100,000 free subscribers on roughly $1M raised total, where 99% of code is written by coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Droid) and each app is built by a single developer. He names the resulting practice "compounding engineering" — a four-step loop of plan, delegate, assess, codify, where the codify step turns lessons from each cycle into explicit prompts stored in CLAUDE.md files, subagents, and slash commands so each feature makes the next one easier rather than harder. He lists concrete second-order effects he's observed: developers reimplement a teammate's feature in a different stack by pointing their agent at that repo instead of writing a shared library, new hires are productive on day one because environment setup and PR conventions live in agent config files, and even non-engineers (himself as CEO) can commit production code by delegating investigation to an agent between meetings.

## Why it matters
- Gives a named, concrete practice ("compounding engineering": plan/delegate/assess/codify) for turning per-task AI usage into an organizational capability, with the "codify" step as the specific mechanism (CLAUDE.md, subagents, slash commands) for making that compounding real rather than aspirational.
- The claim that 90% vs. 100% agent adoption is qualitatively different, not just incrementally faster, is a specific, arguable thesis about organizational design under AI that a book chapter on AI-native teams can engage with directly.
- The reported metrics (99% AI-written code, one engineer per production app, 15 people running four paying products) are a concrete existence proof to weigh against more skeptical claims about AI's current limits on software engineering headcount.
