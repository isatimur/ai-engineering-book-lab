# Disrupting the $15 Trillion Construction Industry with Autonomous Agents: Dr. Sarah Buchner

## Summary
Dr. Sarah Buchner is founder and CEO of Trunk Tools, which she calls the leading generative AI provider for construction. She has a PhD in AI and construction. In a five-minute lightning talk on the RAG stage, she makes a case that construction is the ideal vertical for retrieval, and then says RAG itself is commoditized. The real prize, in her view, is vertical agents that solve real-world problems while the human stays at the center.

**The scale of the data**
A skyscraper under construction in New York City uses the software. When Trunk Tools asked for all its documentation, the building handed over 3.6 million pages: contracts, pictures, blueprints, schedules and RFIs. Printed, the stack would be three times as high as the building. Many people make decisions every day and document them heavily, which is why she says the industry is "literally built for rack" (RAG).

**The cost of discrepancies**
She compares construction to software. Many people write code, and it breaks on merge. In construction the bug shows up in the field, when a pipe does not fit its hole or concrete goes in the wrong place. Construction is $15 trillion a year. About 10% of it is rework caused by data discrepancies nobody saw, which she puts at $1.5 trillion a year. The industry is about 10% of GDP.

**The brain and the agents**
Trunk Tools puts all project knowledge in one place, "the brain behind construction": a RAG product built to digest construction-specific file types. Users ask questions by text message or web app. Within seconds they get enterprise search results, a semantic answer and the source document. Her field example asks whether a specific door needs power-actuated hardware. The answer itself surfaces a discrepancy in the documents, a mistake that would be very expensive. She shows a typical source file: a three-foot-wide table of numbers that no human can use.

On top of the brain sits "an army of AI based agents". Her example: instead of stopping at a flagged discrepancy, an agent creates the RFI and resolves it, so the human can return to the job and not be "a victim of bureaucracy". The description says the company deploys a new agent every 45 days.

## Why it matters
- A strong vertical case for RAG: millions of pages per project, hostile document formats, and a clear dollar cost of missed information.
- Frames retrieval answers as a discrepancy detector, not just a Q&A tool, which changes what success looks like.
- Shows the move from search to action: an agent that files an RFI when the brain finds a conflict.
- A founder's explicit claim that horizontal RAG is commoditized and the value sits in vertical, human-centered agents.
