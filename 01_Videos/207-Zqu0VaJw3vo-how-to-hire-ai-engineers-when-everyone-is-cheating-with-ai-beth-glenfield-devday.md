# How to Hire AI Engineers when EVERYONE is cheating with AI — Beth Glenfield, DevDay

## Summary
Beth Glenfield (DevDay) argues technical interviewing broke over the last 18 months: she cites the AI-cheating startup Cluely (raised $5.3M, heading toward $1M ARR), a claimed 93% success rate for a tool called "LeetCode Wizard" on Google/Meta-style interviews, and an estimate that one in three interviews now involve an AI assistant on the candidate's side — meaning, in her framing, that a LeetCode interview mostly measures who has the better AI coding assistant. She also cites Marc Benioff's statement that Salesforce won't hire software engineers this year, attributed to a claimed 30% productivity boost from replacing people with AI (a figure she says she'd like to see the underlying data for), and Mark Zuckerberg's prediction that AI will handle mid-level engineering work by later in the year. Her proposed alternative, which DevDay is building with design partners, replaces coding-puzzle interviews with workplace simulations: candidates build features in the company's actual business domain while working alongside AI agents given distinct personas (a perfectionist, a pragmatist, a security expert, a junior developer needing mentoring), and are evaluated on how they delegate, handle ambiguity, communicate decisions in PRs and tickets, and mentor — not on raw coding output. She frames this as necessary for smaller companies that, unlike Google or Meta, cannot afford to brute-force hiring by interviewing hundreds of candidates and can lose $20-60K on a single bad hire.
