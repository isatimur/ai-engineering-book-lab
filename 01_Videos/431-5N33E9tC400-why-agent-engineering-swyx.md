# Why Agent Engineering — swyx

## Summary
swyx leads the AI Engineer Summit and uses his short opening talk to mark the state of the industry. His thesis: the job of the AI engineer is evolving towards building agents, in the same way that ML engineers build models and software engineers build software. That is why the Summit became an agent engineering conference.

**AI engineering as its own discipline**
His earlier talks covered the rise of the AI engineer and the discipline maturing across fields. Now he meets resistance from both sides. ML engineers see an ML engineer plus a few prompts. Software engineers see software engineering plus a few LLM API calls. He still says AI engineering is "90% software engineering 10% AI", but expects the AI share to grow and the field to emerge as its own discipline. He treats language as anthropology: ML people say test-time compute, AI engineers say inference-time compute.

**Why a single-topic summit**
The Summit said no to RAG, open models and GPUs as tracks. Last year's top YouTube talks showed demand for agentic content, but the first speakers were mostly people who sell agent frameworks. So a new rule: no vendor pitches, and speakers who put agents in production. He also notes that "everything plus agent Works": agent plus RAG, agent plus search.

**Skepticism and definitions**
He and his co-host were on record as skeptical. In March 2024 they told people to take agents off their branding; now they tell them to put it back on. At the conference's Family Feud game, agents was the number two buzzword people are tired of. For definitions he cites Simon Willison, who crowdsourced 300 of them. They cluster around goals, tools, control flow, long-running processes, delegated authority, and small multi-step task completion. Another option: list things everyone agrees are agents and check that your definition passes them. OpenAI also published a new agent definition the day before.

**Why agents work now**
- Capabilities are starting to hit human baselines, with better reasoning, tool use and tools such as MCP.
- Model diversity: OpenAI market share fell from about 95% two years ago to about 50%, and two new frontier labs emerged that week.
- The cost of GPT-4-level intelligence fell 1,000 times in 18 months, and the same curve is starting for o1-level intelligence.
- RL fine-tuning options, charging for outcomes instead of costs (from one of his podcast conversations), multi-agent work, and faster inference from better hardware.

**Use cases and anti-use cases**
Coding agents and support agents have product-market fit, and he says deep research now does too. His anti-use case: "no more flight booking agents". He also points to ChatGPT at 400 million users, 33% growth in three months. ChatGPT spent a year not growing while it shipped no agentic models. He reads the weekly active user chart as o1 models doubling usage and projects a billion users by year end.

## Why it matters
- Gives a dated snapshot of why one major AI engineering conference narrowed to agents, and the curation rules behind it (no vendor pitches, production speakers).
- The six-way split of agent definitions (goals, tools, control flow, long-running, delegated authority, multi-step tasks) is a compact frame for a chapter on what an agent is.
- The why-now list goes beyond reasoning and tools to model diversity, a 1,000x cost drop and RL fine-tuning, which are useful drivers to cite.
- Ties product growth to reasoning and agent capability, with a falsifiable projection of a billion ChatGPT users by year end.
