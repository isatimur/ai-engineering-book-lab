# 6 Things to Know about AIE World's Fair 2026

## Summary
This is not a technical talk. It is a low-budget, one-take update from one of the AI Engineer co-founders (captioned as Swix), who says a team of 15 now runs the event. He lists six things attendees of the 2026 World's Fair should know. The value for this book is the snapshot of what the organisers think AI engineering covers in 2026.

**1. Bigger, with a new curriculum**
It is the largest AIE yet, with a whole extra day of content; he urges people not to fly out on July 2nd. He describes the event as a curated buffet under one ticket. About 50% of topics are evergreen and the other 50% are new. New or split tracks: auto research, inference, post-training and pre-training (the data quality track), and memory and continual learning. He notes recent events in Melbourne, Miami and Singapore, Paris coming in June, and strong YouTube growth.

**2. The expo as the main event**
He says the talks are almost optional because everything is recorded; the point is the hallway track. The expo at Moscone West is roughly 4x the 2025 size, with four expo stages, robots, and themed street names such as Attention Avenue, Context Crescent and Diffusion Drive. The cheapest Explorer ticket gives full access to the floor.

**3. Research meets industry**
AIE positions itself as the industry counterpart to ICML and NeurIPS. A partnership with the ACM set up a conference on AI and Agentic Systems with an industry spotlight. He wants to update the poster session: anyone can submit a hot-take tweet, which is printed and defended like a paper. His argument is that curated, high-effort communities beat recommendation feeds that serve ten copies of what you liked once.

**4. Leadership**
The whole of level three, about 50% of breakout space, goes to the leadership track. A token billionaire lounge is for people spending on the order of a billion tokens a month; he says some spend 10 trillion. He expects an even split between those trying to spend more wisely and those trying to spend less. Daily themes respond to leaders who want real workflows from people not selling anything, token maxing, and AI factories. There is bookable off-the-record networking, and McKinsey runs a daily session.

**5. Verticals over horizontals**
Last year added broad tracks such as design engineering and product management. This year bets on verticals: forward deployed engineering, agentic commerce, healthcare, AI in GTM, and above all finance. He pre-announces a third AI Engineer New York, the first focused entirely on finance. He calls finance, from his own background as a trader, the vertical closest to takeoff after code.

**6. Side events**
41 side events were listed at recording, including a World Cup watch, a kids event set up by Neo4j, an opening-night dating event, and a first new engineer orientation (NEO) for solo or first-time attendees, with 300 sign-ups so far. He closes on community over slop.

## Why it matters
- Records how the organisers carve up AI engineering in 2026: auto research, memory and continual learning, and split inference and post-training tracks are now first-class topics.

- Signals the shift from horizontal roles to vertical deployment, with finance named as the next domain after code.
