# Leadership in AI Assisted Engineering – Justin Reock, DX (acq. Atlassian)

## Summary
Justin Reock (DX, acquired by Atlassian) opens by contrasting conflicting productivity data: the METR study found a 19% productivity decrease from AI coding assistants even though every participating engineer felt more productive, while DORA's industry-average data shows only modest gains (7.5% better documentation quality, 3.4% better code quality) from a 25% increase in AI adoption — but DX's own per-company breakdowns show massive variance, with some organizations seeing 20% gains in change confidence and others 20% losses. He attributes the divide to top-down adoption mandates and lack of enablement, and pitches DX's "utilization, impact, cost" measurement framework (mixing telemetry, in-workflow experience sampling, and survey data) as the fix, citing Google's Project Aristotle finding that psychological safety, not raw talent, predicted team performance. He backs the "augment, don't replace" framing with SWE-bench data showing agents complete only about a third of assigned tasks unassisted, and closes with named case studies: Zapier cut new-engineer ramp time to 2 weeks (vs. an industry benchmark of 30-90 days) and responded by hiring faster rather than cutting headcount, Morgan Stanley's "Dev Gen AI" tool is saving roughly 300,000 engineer-hours a year modernizing legacy COBOL/mainframe/Natural/Perl code, and Spotify uses AI to push incident context and runbook steps directly into SRE channels to cut MTTR.

## Why it matters
