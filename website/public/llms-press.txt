## The Model Layer & The Long Tail: Training, Inference, and the Domains Beyond Text

An early, source-anchored draft on how AI engineering is reshaping training, inference, and the long tail of domains beyond text. Status: Drafting (8 chapters · 17,316 words · 43 claims · 88 anchors).

- [The Model Layer & The Long Tail](https://aiepress.com/second-book): the book's home page.
- [Chapter 1: Training and the Turn to RL](https://aiepress.com/second-book/the-turn-to-rl): Why the center of gravity in model-building moved from pre-training to post-training — and reinforcement learning above all.
- [Chapter 2: Inference Economics](https://aiepress.com/second-book/inference-economics): Why serving a model, not training it, is where the cost, the latency, and most AI engineering careers actually live.
- [Chapter 3: Building Frontier Models](https://aiepress.com/second-book/frontier-models): Why building a frontier model became its own engineering culture, and why the frontier is now crowded rather than a two-lab race.
- [Chapter 4: Beyond Text: Models for Speech, Media, Perception, and Action](https://aiepress.com/second-book/beyond-text): How speech, media, recommendation, driving, and embodiment models are all converging on the same LLM-shaped design.
- [Chapter 5: Robotics and the Physical World](https://aiepress.com/second-book/robotics): Why robotics is not "agents plus a body" — the physical world adds constraints the standard harness never has to handle.
- [Chapter 6: Regulated and High-Stakes Domains](https://aiepress.com/second-book/high-stakes-domains): Why finance, legal, insurance, and medicine need a different engineering posture, not just a smarter general model.
- [Chapter 7: Creative, Education, and Games](https://aiepress.com/second-book/creative-and-games): Why creative, educational, and game applications keep the model in a narrow lane while structured systems hold the ground truth.
- [Chapter 8: What the Edges Teach](https://aiepress.com/second-book/what-the-edges-teach): What the model layer and the long tail say together: capability consolidates, accountability does not, and the constraints were never compensation for a weak model.

## The Shape of the Work: When Writing the Code Stops Being the Constraint

Spec-driven development and the software factory: what happens to the lifecycle when writing the code stops being the constraint. Status: First draft (12 of 12 chapters · 133 talks classified · spec published).

- [The Shape of the Work](https://aiepress.com/third-book): the book's home page.
- [Chapter 1: The Bottleneck Moved](https://aiepress.com/third-book/the-bottleneck-moved): Why "code generation was never the bottleneck" reframes the whole lifecycle — and why the feeling of going faster is not evidence that you are.
- [Chapter 2: Intent Before Specification](https://aiepress.com/third-book/intent-before-specification): What has to be settled before any document helps — and why the instinct to specify first fails on capabilities nobody has mapped yet.
- [Chapter 3: What a Specification Is For](https://aiepress.com/third-book/what-a-specification-is-for): Code is a lossy projection of the reasoning that produced it. A specification is the surface you argue on before that reasoning is lost.
- [Chapter 4: Decomposition and the Plan](https://aiepress.com/third-book/decomposition-and-the-plan): Why the size of a planned step is a context constraint rather than a matter of taste, and why a bad line in a plan costs a hundred bad lines of code.
- [Chapter 5: Execution Under a Spec](https://aiepress.com/third-book/execution-under-a-spec): A specification binds through exactly two mechanisms — what the agent can reach, and what gets checked afterwards. Everything else is advisory.
- [Chapter 6: The Independence Problem](https://aiepress.com/third-book/the-independence-problem): A judge that nearly doubled a benchmark score, and why the most dangerous gate in your pipeline is the one you wrote yourself.
- [Chapter 7: Gates That Can Actually Fail](https://aiepress.com/third-book/gates-that-can-actually-fail): Gates die two ways: crying wolf, or never objecting. The second is silent, gets praised, and is far more dangerous.
- [Chapter 8: Shipping, and the Gap Between Merged and Live](https://aiepress.com/third-book/shipping-and-the-gap-between-merged-and-live): Thirty consecutive commits that deployed nothing while every indicator said success — and why an absence produces no artifact.
- [Chapter 9: Brownfield and Spec Rot](https://aiepress.com/third-book/brownfield-and-spec-rot): When the system is too large to hold in view, every slice is locally correct and the whole drifts. Three different decays hide under one phrase.
- [Chapter 10: The Review Wall](https://aiepress.com/third-book/the-review-wall): Lines of code up 2,450%. Commits up 240%. Releases up 30%. The gap is inventory, and "human in the loop" is where it gets relabelled as control.
- [Chapter 11: The Factory and Its Discontents](https://aiepress.com/third-book/the-factory-and-its-discontents): A factory makes the same thing repeatedly. Software never makes the same thing twice — which is why the metaphor works for some work and strains everywhere else.
- [Chapter 12: How Much Process, and How Far Out of the Loop](https://aiepress.com/third-book/how-much-process-and-how-far-out-of-the-loop): Two measured rounds where ceremony cost a third more tokens for an identical scorecard — and the one result no scorecard could see.
